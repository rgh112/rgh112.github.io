Research directions

01Representation & generalization

Learn what carries over.

Which relations must a model preserve to reason in a new situation?

I’m particularly interested in how models represent relationships and changes in their environment. A central question is whether these representations support prediction and planning when familiar elements appear in unfamiliar combinations.

Possible approaches

Vary the learned representation and prediction objective, then test unseen combinations of relations and changes in the environment.

What to evaluate

Transfer, predictive accuracy, and the quality of downstream decisions.

Where it connects

Planning depends on representations that preserve how actions change a situation. Testing transfer can help reveal which of those relationships a model has learned.

02World models & planning

Anticipate what an action changes.

Can an agent recognize when its next move would close off its goal?

Scientific exploration often proceeds through edits: change a composition, modify a sequence, evaluate the result. My interest is in how agents learn to anticipate the options each edit opens or closes.

Possible approaches

Begin with environments where goal reachability can be checked, then introduce partial observations and changing constraints.

What to evaluate

Goal attainment, preservation of feasible paths, constraint violations, and computational cost.

Where it connects

Predicting the consequences of an action requires tracking both the current state and the options that remain. This connects world models with planning under constraints.

03Information & collaboration

Know what to ask next.

When should an agent observe, consult another agent, or ask a person?

A useful decision may depend on information the agent does not yet have. I’m interested in when gathering more information improves a decision, and how an agent can judge what is worth asking.

Possible approaches

Control what is visible and remembered; compare additional observations, peer evidence, and expert input under a shared cost budget.

What to evaluate

Decision improvement, information cost, correction of errors, and whether people can understand and act on requests.

Where it connects

Information gathering is itself a decision: an observation, another agent, or a person can each change the next step. This connects active learning with collaboration and human–AI interaction.

Where these questions matter

Scientific discovery.
Autonomous systems.
Human judgment.

Scientific work requires evidence from different sources. Autonomous systems turn predictions into sequences of actions. In both, a person may need to inspect a decision or intervene. These settings offer ways to examine how learning, reasoning, and planning work together.
